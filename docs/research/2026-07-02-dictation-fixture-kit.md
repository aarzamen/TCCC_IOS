# Dictation Fixture Kit — human-voice benchmark recordings

**Purpose:** three ~3-minute medic narrations, read by a real human (you), on the
real target device, to serve as the app's representative ASR benchmark corpus.
The scripts below ARE the ground truth — read them verbatim and WER scoring is
exact by construction. TTS fixtures (Kokoro) remain the *controlled* lane;
these are the *representative* lane — the "reasonable person standard" made
operational.

## Recording protocol (2 minutes to read, then go)

1. **Device:** the iPhone 17 Pro, **Voice Memos**, phone in hand or propped at
   chest distance (roughly 20–30 cm from your mouth) — that's the app's real
   geometry.
2. **Room:** normal indoor room. Don't chase silence and don't add noise —
   ordinary ambient is the point. (Noise overlays come later, in software.)
3. **Delivery:** natural medic cadence — steady, purposeful, unhurried. Brief
   pauses between MARCH phases are good. Don't perform it; report it.
4. **Stay on script.** WER needs a verbatim reference. If you flub a word,
   just keep going (don't restart, don't re-read the sentence) — a couple of
   real-world flubs are fine and we'll note them; restarts poison the timing.
5. **One take per script.** Name the memos exactly: **Fixture Alpha**,
   **Fixture Bravo**, **Fixture Charlie**.
6. **Send:** share each .m4a the same way as before (Voice Memos → share; the
   tmp path lands in chat) or AirDrop all three to the Desktop and say so.

Numbers are written the way they should be *spoken* (grids and clock times as
single digits, vitals as plain numbers). Say them as printed.

## Fixture Alpha — IED blast, bilateral lower-extremity hemorrhage (~3 min)

This is Atlas One-Three with a MEDEVAC request. Line one, grid Whiskey Delta
four one seven two, three zero niner eight. Line two, four five point five zero,
call sign Atlas. Line three, one urgent surgical. Line four, none. Line six,
one litter, urgent.

Casualty is US military, battle roster Alpha Mike two two zero five. No known
drug allergies. Mechanism of injury is an IED blast, dismounted patrol. Time of
injury zero eight one five. He has traumatic injury to both legs below the knee
with massive hemorrhage from the right side.

Massive hemorrhage first. Bright red spurting blood from the right lower leg.
Applying a CAT tourniquet to the right thigh, high and tight. Tourniquet time
zero eight one seven. Still oozing from the left calf, applying a second
tourniquet to the left thigh. Second tourniquet time zero eight one nine.
Bleeding is controlled now, both sides. Marking times on the tourniquets.

Airway. He is screaming, so his airway is open. He is alert and responding to
me by name.

Respirations. Breathing is fast but effective, respiratory rate is 24. Breath
sounds are equal on both sides. No chest wounds. Placing him on the monitor.

Circulation. Radial pulse is present and rapid. Heart rate is 130. Blood
pressure is 85 over 50. Oxygen saturation is 95 percent. Starting an 18 gauge
IV in the right AC. Giving 2 grams of tranexamic acid by slow IV push now.
Starting 500 milliliters of Hextend for hypotension.

Head and hypothermia. Pupils are equal and reactive. No head trauma. He never
lost consciousness. Wrapping him in a hypothermia blanket and getting him onto
a litter, off the ground.

Pain and antibiotics. Giving 50 milligrams of ketamine IM for pain at zero
eight two five. For antibiotics, 1 gram of ertapenem IV.

Reassessment at zero eight three five. Heart rate has come down to 118. Blood
pressure is up to 95 over 60. Oxygen saturation is 96 percent. Both tourniquets
are holding, no breakthrough bleeding. Dressings are dry. He remains alert.
Splinting the left leg for transport. Casualty is packaged, tourniquet times
are marked on his forehead, documentation is going with him. Standing by for
DUSTOFF. Atlas One-Three, end of report.

## Fixture Bravo — GSW right chest, needle decompression (~3 min)

This is Ranger Four-One reporting on one casualty. US military, battle roster
Bravo Kilo three three one eight. Allergy to penicillin. Mechanism is small
arms fire, a single gunshot wound to the right chest, entry wound only. Time
of injury one four zero five.

Massive hemorrhage. No massive external bleeding. The chest wound is not
bleeding heavily on the outside.

Airway is patent. He is talking in short sentences and he is anxious.

Respirations. He is breathing fast and shallow, respiratory rate is 30. Breath
sounds are diminished on the right side. Placing a vented chest seal over the
entry wound now. Checking his back — no exit wound. He is getting worse, more
short of breath, and his oxygen saturation is dropping. It reads 88 percent.
Trachea is still midline but I am not waiting. Performing a needle
decompression, right side, second intercostal space, midclavicular line. I got
a rush of air. His breathing is easier already.

Circulation. Radial pulse present, heart rate is 125. Blood pressure is 100
over 70. Oxygen saturation is coming back up, now reading 94 percent. Starting
an 18 gauge IV in the left forearm. Giving 1 gram of tranexamic acid slow IV
push.

Head and hypothermia. He is alert and oriented. Pupils equal and reactive.
Keeping him warm with a blanket, sitting him up slightly to help his breathing.

Pain and antibiotics. Giving 30 milligrams of ketamine slow IV for pain at one
four one five. Because of the penicillin allergy I am holding ertapenem and
giving moxifloxacin 400 milligrams by mouth instead — he can swallow.

Reassessment at one four two five. Respiratory rate is down to 22. Heart rate
is 110. Blood pressure is 105 over 72. Oxygen saturation is 94 percent and
holding. Chest seal is intact, no reaccumulation so far. I will burp the seal
if he gets worse and I am ready to decompress again if tension physiology
returns. Requesting urgent surgical evacuation, one litter. Monitoring
continuously. Ranger Four-One, end of report.

## Fixture Charlie — vehicle rollover, head injury and femur fracture (~3 min)

This is Havoc Two-Two with one casualty from a vehicle rollover. US military,
battle roster Charlie Papa five five six one. No known drug allergies.
Mechanism is blunt trauma, an MRAP rollover down an embankment. He was
restrained. Time of injury one six three zero.

Massive hemorrhage. No massive external bleeding. There is a deformity of the
right thigh consistent with a femur fracture, swelling but no open wound.

Airway. He responds to voice but he is confused. Airway is open, he is
handling his own secretions. Placing a nasopharyngeal airway as a precaution.
He tolerates it.

Respirations. Breathing is regular, respiratory rate is 18. Breath sounds
equal on both sides. No chest wall injury that I can find.

Circulation. Radial pulse is strong, heart rate is 96. Blood pressure is 130
over 85. Oxygen saturation is 98 percent. He has good color. Starting a saline
lock, 18 gauge, left hand.

Head injury check. He is confused about events and repeating questions. AVPU is
Verbal. He has a scalp laceration over the left eye, bleeding controlled with a
pressure dressing. Pupils are equal and reactive but sluggish. I am treating
this as a traumatic brain injury. Because of the head injury I am holding
ketamine.

Hypothermia. Wrapping him in a blanket, keeping him flat with the head slightly
elevated.

Pain, antibiotics, splinting. For pain, acetaminophen 1000 milligrams by
mouth — he can swallow with supervision. No antibiotics indicated, no open
wounds beyond the scalp laceration. Applying a traction splint to the right
femur now. Distal pulse present before and after splinting.

Reassessment at one six five zero. He is more alert, answering questions
correctly now. Heart rate is 92. Blood pressure is 128 over 82. Respiratory
rate is 16. Oxygen saturation is 98 percent. Pupils remain equal, still
sluggish on the left. Splint is holding, distal pulse intact. This is a
priority evacuation, one litter, non-ambulatory, with traumatic brain injury
precautions — keep him warm, keep the head up, no narcotics on the bird if
avoidable. Havoc Two-Two, end of report.

## What happens on receipt (my side)

1. Each memo becomes a fixture: `fixture_alpha` / `fixture_bravo` /
   `fixture_charlie` — the script text above (verbatim) becomes the `.txt`
   reference, plus a `.json` sidecar with per-scenario field expectations
   (vitals trends, interventions, meds).
2. Benchmark runs on-device. Known caveat: the **current** lane will show its
   long-form truncation (tail-only) — these fixtures' full value activates
   with the WS-3 SpeechAnalyzer lane, and they also serve the WS-2 acoustic
   replay A/B and every future backend bake-off. Human-voice fixtures never
   expire.
3. Any read-flubs you tell me about get annotated in the sidecar as reference
   defects (same policy as the TTS lane — we don't tune the scorer to hide
   reality).

## Design notes (why these three)

- **Coverage spread:** Alpha = M-heavy (dual tourniquet, TXA 2 g, Hextend);
  Bravo = R-heavy (chest seal, needle decompression, allergy-driven med
  substitution); Charlie = H-heavy (TBI, AVPU Verbal, ketamine contraindication,
  traction splint, priority-not-urgent). Together they exercise most of the
  MARCH/PAWS vocabulary and DD-1380 fields the engine extracts, without
  reusing the existing GSW-thigh/tourniquet-conversion scenario.
- **Reasonable-person calibration:** single speaker, purposeful pace, ordinary
  room. Representative, not adversarial. Degraded-condition variants (noise
  overlay, faster speech) are software transforms on these same recordings
  later — one clean master beats many noisy takes.
